# X reach

Write X posts the way posts that actually get recommended are written. The numbers below come from a public study of 2,194 video posts from 300 accounts (Aug to Nov 2026) and from the author's own tests. Treat them as observations, not laws.

## What the data says

- Followers barely predict how big a post gets (correlation about 0.05). The recommendation feed makes hits, so a small account is no excuse.
- About 10% of posts bring 50-95% of all views. A hit is a probability, so post consistently.
- Native video posts hit 30% of the time, posts without video 16%. The sweet spot is about 100 seconds; under 60 seconds does worse.
- Text of 1,000-1,500 characters in short paragraphs beats short text. Under 600 characters does about 30% worse.
- A hook with a person, a number and a twist on line 1 hits 26% of the time vs 15%.
- Pointing at the video on line 2 ("In the video below...") hits 32% vs 17%.
- 82% of viral posts end with a question that has two sides.
- The first person to post a story gets about 4.6x their usual views; a repeat gets 1.2x.
- After a hit, the next three posts average about 2.45x. Have posts ready.
- Viral posts get fewer likes per view, because strangers watch them. Don't judge a post by early likes.
- Time of day was within noise in this data.

## Post template

1. HOOK (about 150 characters): who + a real number + the twist. Add the year if there is one.
2. VIDEO: "In the video below, ..." (say what the viewer will see).
3. FACT with a number.
4. CONFLICT: the second fact that makes it worse or stranger.
5. THE NUMBER NOBODY EXPECTED.
6. YOUR TAKE: one or two lines only you could write. This is what makes the post original.
7. QUESTION with two sides: "Was it X, or Y?"

## "I made it, here's the result" template (build in public, AI, tools)

1. Belief flip: "Most people think ..."
2. What you did, with the real time and constraints ("I gave Claude Code 3 minutes 38 seconds and told it not to install anything").
3. What it found or made, as 3 bullets with real numbers.
4. What it got wrong or couldn't do. Honest misses drive comments.
5. "What yours might show": 3-7 possible outcomes, so the post is useful even if the reader's result differs.
6. "Prompt in the first reply." Then post the prompt and steps as your own first reply right away.
7. Ask readers to share their own result. A day or two later, post "here's what people found" from real replies.

## Rules

- Every number, time and outcome must match what really happened. A post with a Community Note loses reach and monetization.
- No invented first-person stories. No results before the real run.
- Don't copy someone else's text: verbatim copies get almost no reach (8.0M original vs 1.1K copy in the study). Add your own take.
- Re-uploading someone's video without your own text doesn't count as original. Credit the source.
- No engagement pods, bought accounts or bots.
- Links in replies under other people's posts hurt; say "prompt's in my pinned post" instead.

## Replies (growth)

- 10-15 strong replies a day under fresh posts (first 30-60 minutes) in your niche from accounts with 1k-100k followers.
- Each reply adds something: a number from your own test, a tip, a short counterpoint or a question. Never "great post".
- Vary the shape and length. Never the same opener twice.

## Review checklist

Before posting, check:
- [ ] Line 1 has a real number and a twist
- [ ] Video attached (60-180 s) or a strong visual
- [ ] 1,000-1,500 characters, short paragraphs
- [ ] Your own take is in there
- [ ] Every fact can be checked
- [ ] Ends with a two-sided question
- [ ] Prompt, source or link goes in the first reply, not the post
